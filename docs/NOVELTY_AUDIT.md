
# FieldTwin — Scientific Novelty Audit

**Version:** 1.0
**Execution Plan:** FieldTwin v6.2
**Status:** Bounded literature review recorded; individual pilot-site novelty remains insufficiently evidenced.

## 1. Purpose

This document evaluates the scientific novelty of Earth analog locations and mission-specific applications proposed by FieldTwin.

Its purpose is to prevent unsupported discovery claims and establish whether the project's contribution is genuinely differentiated from existing research.

No first-ever discovery or new analog location is currently claimed.

## 2. Novelty Research Questions

For each leading candidate, investigate:

1. Has the Earth location previously been studied?
2. Has it been identified as a Moon or Mars analog?
3. Has it been used in rover mobility or navigation research?
4. Which terrain characteristics have previously been measured?
5. Has a similar Mars–Earth terrain comparison already been published?
6. Does FieldTwin introduce a different comparison method?
7. Does FieldTwin propose a previously undocumented rover-testing application?
8. What evidence supports the proposed contribution?

## 3. Types of Novelty

### A. Known Analog

An Earth location already documented as a planetary analog.

The project may still contribute a new analysis or engineering application.

### B. Under-Recognized Candidate

A location with evidence supporting potential analog relevance but limited documented use for the selected mission objective.

This does not establish that the location was previously undiscovered.

### C. New Mission-Specific Use

A known or previously studied location proposed for a different, technically justified Mars rover-testing purpose.

The proposed use must be supported by measured terrain properties and a review of previous applications.

### D. Insufficient Evidence

Available literature and observations are not sufficient to establish novelty.

Do not make a novelty claim in this case.

## 9. Methodological Novelty

Evaluate whether FieldTwin offers a meaningful methodological contribution.

Potential contributions include:

- Comparing Earth and Mars terrain at compatible effective physical scales.
- Combining interpretable terrain-feature ranking with documented uncertainty.
- Evaluating analog suitability for a specified rover-testing task.
- Using a known analog and negative control.
- Reporting an explicit Analog Transfer Matrix.
- Benchmarking optional ML against a physical-feature baseline.

These are intended project features, not automatically novel research contributions.

A novelty claim requires evidence that the particular method, combination, or application differs meaningfully from existing work.

## 10. Scientific Differentiation

For each leading candidate, answer:

1. What has already been established by earlier research?
2. What does FieldTwin measure or evaluate differently?
3. What new engineering insight is proposed?
4. How does that insight support Mars exploration?
5. What scientific evidence supports the distinction?
6. What important uncertainty remains?

Do not use visual appearance alone as evidence of differentiation.
